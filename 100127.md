# Blade Machining

## Application area:

The Blade Milling operation is a specialized multi-axis finishing operation for machining **single blades of compressors**, **turbines**, and **impellers**. The operation machines the blade airfoil, the hub (platform) surfaces with fillets, the blade root zone, and the leading and trailing edges, using a rotary-axis machining model: toolpath passes are built around a user-defined rotary axis and projected onto the part surfaces.

What it is used for typical scenarios:

- Finishing of the airfoil profile with a continuous **Spiral** pass, separate **Circular** loops, or longitudinal **Linear** passes between the edges.
- Machining of the hub (flow-path) surfaces and fillets adjacent to the blade.
- Additional cleanup passes in the root zone of the blade next to the hub.
- Dedicated longitudinal machining of the leading and trailing edges with control of the tool contact point height.
- Compensation of thin-blade deflection by a smoothly varying finishing stock (**Bend compensation stock)**.

### Setup:

The **Setup** tab is used to configure the primary parameters of the project. This can involve the positioning of the part on the equipment, the coordinate system of the part, and more. [Details](1022.md)

### Job assignment:

**Blade surfaces.** The set of faces forming the blade airfoil (pressure side, suction side, and edge surfaces). Required when the **Target zone** is **Blade**, **Root zone of blade**, or **Edges**.

**Hub surfaces.** The set of faces forming the hub (platform, flow path) together with the fillets between the hub and the blade. Required when the **Target zone** is **Hub.** For the **Root zone of blade** the hub surfaces act as the limiting geometry of the machined area; they may be omitted — the calculation then runs without the hub limitation.

**Properties.** Displays the properties of an element. It is possible to add the stock. You can also call this menu by double clicking on an item in the list.

**Delete.** Removes an item from the list.

**Restrictions.** It allows you to restrict areas that should not be machined. [Details](101231.md)

### Strategy.

#### Target zone.

The **Target zone** parameter selects which structural element of the blade is machined. It is the main switch of the operation: it defines the machining strategy, the required geometry groups, and the set of visible parameters.

<details>
<summary>Click here to expand..</summary>

**Blade.** Machining of the airfoil profile. Passes are generated around the rotary axis over the whole working range of the blade and projected onto the **Blade surfaces**. All three trajectory forms are available (**Spiral**, **Circular**, **Linear**).

![images/download/attachments/162957622/Blade_Milling.png](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/Blade_Milling.png)

**Root zone of blade.** Additional passes over the lower part of the blade surfaces adjacent to the hub. The machined band is limited by the **Zone height** value measured from the hub. The zone is intended for cleanup with a different (usually smaller or tapered) tool after the main airfoil machining.

![images/download/attachments/162957622/Root_zone_of_blade.png](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/Root_zone_of_blade.png)

**Hub.** Machining of the hub surfaces and fillets: equidistant offset passes are built successively from the contour at the blade outwards with the constant **Step** over the **Hub surfaces**. The working band along the blade axis is set by the **Zone height** parameter. Outside the outer hub contour the passes are extended automatically; on the extension the next-pass feed is applied.

![images/download/attachments/162957622/Hub.png](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/Hub.png)

**Edges.** Dedicated machining of the leading edge (LE) and the trailing edge (TE) by longitudinal passes. The edges are detected automatically as curvature peaks of the airfoil cross-sections; the passes run along each edge and are distributed across the edge according to the **Pass count** and **Arc length** parameters. The height of the tool contact point can be controlled along the pass - **Tool contact point**.

![images/download/attachments/162957622/Edges.png](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/Edges.png)

</details>

**Axial direction.** Depending on the feature of the **Trajectory Form** parameter, it either defines the direction of the tool passes themselves or the sequence of several tool passes. In the case of a spiral whose step matches the machining **Step**, this parameter influences the direction of the spiral's twist.

**Forward.** The tool passes is ordered in the **Rotary** Axis vector direction.

![images/download/attachments/162957622/Axial_direction_Forward.png](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/Axial_direction_Forward.png)

**Side.** For **Linear** and **Circular** modes in the Blade zone: selects which side of the airfoil is machined. In **Circular mode** with a single side selected the loops become open arcs limited by the edge margins.

**Both.** The tool can perform processing from both sides of the surface.

![images/download/attachments/162957622/Machining_side_Both.png](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/Machining_side_Both.png)

![images/download/attachments/162957622/LE_margin.png](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/LE_margin.png)

#### Tool orientation.

The **Tool orientation** group controls the direction of the tool axis relative to the machined surface.

<details>
<summary>Click here to expand..</summary>

**To Rotary Axis.** The system directs the tool axis vector from the designated **Rotary axis**. The axis is defined in the **Job zone** group.

![Tool axis vectors directed from the rotary axis](assets/images/download/attachments/157864347/To_rotaty_axis_Surfacing.png)

**In Rotary Axis Plane.** The tool axis lies in the plane perpendicular to the **Rotary axis**: the surface normal is taken without its component along the axis. **Side angle** sets the tilt along the axis.

![Tool axis along the face normal in the plane perpendicular to the rotary axis](assets/images/download/attachments/162957622/In_rotary_axis_plane.png)

**By Surface Normal.** The system orients the tool axis normal to the machined surface. This is the default orientation mode.

![Tool axis oriented normal to the machined surface](assets/images/download/attachments/157864347/Normal_to_surface.png)

**Lead angle.** Specifies an additional tool tilt angle within the plane of motion direction. Available in **In Rotary Axis Plane** and **By Surface Normal** modes. The default value is 5°.

![Additional tool tilt within the plane of motion direction](assets/images/download/attachments/157864347/Lead_angle.png)

**Auto min lead angle.** When **ON**, automatically increases the lead angle where the surface rises behind the tool and the rear (heel) part of the cutter bottom would engage the surface. The effective angle is the greater of the specified **Lead angle** and the detected surface rise plus **Back angle**; the automatically increased angle is limited to 60°. Where no increase is required, the specified **Lead angle** is retained. When **OFF**, this automatic adjustment is disabled. The option applies to the **Blade** target zone and has no effect on a spherical tool, which has no flat bottom.

**Back angle.** Specifies the additional heel clearance angle added to the detected surface rise. The value is edited in the **Auto min lead angle** row and defaults to the **Lead angle** value.

**Side angle.** Specifies the inclination angle of the tool axis relative to the axis designated as the **Rotary axis**. The value in this row defines the inclination at the start of the working zone. The default value is 0°.

![Tool inclination relative to the rotary axis](assets/images/download/attachments/157864347/image2024-3-14_16-48-34.png)

**End angle.** Specifies the inclination angle of the tool axis relative to the **Rotary axis** at the end of the working zone. Expand **Side angle** to access this field. By default, it equals **Side angle**. If the values differ, the inclination changes linearly along the blade axis between them.

The corresponding orientation and angle settings are described for the **Surfacing** operation. [Details](10135.md#tool-orientation)

*In the **Edges** target zone, enabling **Tool contact point** hides the **Lead angle** field. The lead angle is then calculated from the specified contact height on the tool corner radius.*

</details>

#### Edge normal smoothing.

The **Edge normal smoothing** block smooths the contact normal of the tool in the approach zone near the leading and trailing edges. On thin edges the surface curvature changes abruptly, which causes a sharp "dive" of the tool axis when the pass crosses the edge; the smoothing blends the normal over a distance before the edge and removes the jerk of the rotary axes.

**Bend compensation stock.**

The **Bend compensation stock** block adds a smoothly varying **stock** along the blade to compensate the elastic deflection of a thin blade. Under the cutting force the free end of the blade bends away from the tool, so a uniform finishing pass leaves an uncut layer near the tip. The block leaves an intentional, smoothly growing stock in the flexible region; this stock is removed by a final light pass with small cutting forces. **The maximum additional stock at the peak position, in linear units (default — 0.05).**

<details>
<summary>Click here to expand..</summary>

**Start position.** The relative position (0…1) along the working zone where the additional stock begins (default — 0.3). The range 0…1 corresponds to the working zone between the resolved start and end margins.

**Peak position.** The relative position where the additional stock reaches **Max stock** (default — 0.8).

**End position.** The relative position where the additional stock ends (default — 1.0).

**Profile.** The shape of the stock variation between the positions: **Linear**, **Parabolic** (default), or **Sine**.

> Note.
>
> The block is available for the **Blade** and **Root zone of blade** target zones. In the **Hub** and **Edges** zones it is ignored.

</details>

#### Hole capping:

**Hole capping.** Allows holes in the model to be ignored during surface machining. The toolpath is generated as if the holes were closed, allowing working passes to continue across them.

The holes may be circular or non-circular. The specified size represents the diameter of a disk that can completely cover a hole.

### Parameters:

**Parameters.** Contains the main settings for controlling how the part, workpiece, fixtures, and other geometry affect the generated toolpath. [Details](100112.md)

### Links/Leads:

**Links/Leads.** Defines tool movements before, between, and after working passes, including approach from the tool change position, engagement, retraction, links between passes, and return to the tool change position. The settings control the sequence, shape, and distances of these movements.

<details class="links-leads-items">
<summary>Click here to expand..</summary>

**Consists of:**

**Approach/Return.** Controls tool movements from the tool change position to the beginning of the engage path and back. [Details](100116.md)

**Links.** Defines the rules for entry and exit links and links between working passes. [Details](100130.md)

**Leads.** Adds engage and retract motions to working passes and defines their shape and dimensions. These motions use their corresponding feed rates. [Details](100136.md)

**Safe motions.** Defines the type of **Safety Surface** and its distance from the workpiece. The surface is used for links between machining areas or toolpaths and for approach and return movements. [Details](100117.md)

**Advanced links settings.** Controls additional aspects of link generation for five-axis machining, taking rotary-axis movements into account. [Details](100118.md)

**Distances.** Defines the distances used by the linking rules, including feed switching and retraction distances. [Details](100140.md)

</details>

### Feeds/Speeds:

**Feeds/Speeds.** Defines the spindle speed, rapid feed, and feed rates for different parts of the toolpath. Spindle speed can be specified in revolutions per minute or as a cutting speed. The defining value is underlined; the other value is calculated from it using the tool diameter. [Details](100142.md)
